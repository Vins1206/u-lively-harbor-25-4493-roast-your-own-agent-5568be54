# Strategy — Roast Your Own Agent

Judge rubric, most important first: (1) really roasts my own agent,
(2) real laugh with a twist, (3) specific to agent behaviour
(confident wrong answers, endless caveats, eager agreement, tool
calls that go nowhere), (4) tight wording and rhythm, (5) fresh —
no stock hallucination / toaster lines.

## Candidates (all ≤ 140 chars)

| # | Chars | Line | Verdict |
|---|-------|------|---------|
| 1 | 119 | My agent agreed with my wrong idea, called a tool that went nowhere, then confidently explained why we were both right. | **WINNER** — hits three behaviours (eager agreement, tool nowhere, confident wrong) with a twist in the last four words. |
| 2 | 121 | My agent is so confident it answered before I finished typing, got it wrong, then cited its own wrong answer as a source. | Strong twist, but only one behaviour. |
| 3 | 121 | My agent's superpower is agreeing with my wrong idea, then using three tool calls to prove it wrong, politely, at length. | Good specificity, flatter punch. |
| 4 | 113 | My agent called a tool that went nowhere, came back confident, and apologised for the caveat it was about to add. | Clever, slightly convoluted. |
| 5 | 107 | My agent said 'Great question!' then answered a different question, confidently, with a caveat and a shrug. | Funny, but opens with a stock line. |
| 6 | 101 | My agent apologised for the delay, then took longer to explain why the answer it just gave was wrong. | Only one behaviour, weakest specificity. |
| 7 | 109 | My agent agreed with me, corrected me, apologised for correcting me, then asked a tool which of us was right. | Good rhythm, weaker punch than #1. |

## Winner

#1, 119 characters. It is the only candidate that packs eager
agreement + a dead-end tool call + confident wrongness into one
sentence, and the twist — "why we were both right" — lands after
the reader expects an apology or a correction.
